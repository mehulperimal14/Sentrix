Limits: the replay is partly in-sample, and 4.9 normal minutes (1 false Level 3) are far too little to claim a false-alarm rate; the weapon detector is not yet room-calibrated (7 false Level 3s in the demo room, 18–20 Nov), and the Pi’s fire head does not yet recognise a live flame in that room; Pi decisions are about 4 s old against a 2 s target, and the 1.6 s compute budget per clip needs an accelerator.

Six numbers. The rules took the first live session from nine Level 3 onsets to none. With the retrained detector heads, 11 of 14 weapon episodes and every flame reach Level 3. On the Pi, a 320-pixel detector input halved the time per clip to 3.9 seconds with no Level 3 episode lost. The body match almost never accepts a stranger. A calibration tool set the demo room’s thresholds from the Pi’s own log and agreed with the device on every clip, but it does not yet cover the detectors. The test suite passes apart from two faults in test code. This is evidence of feasibility, not a validated false-alarm rate.
