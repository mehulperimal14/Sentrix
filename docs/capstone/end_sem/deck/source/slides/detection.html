08 · How it works

A model score is never an alarm on its own

1 · Confirm

A source counts only after crossing its threshold on 2 of its last 3 fresh evaluations

2 · Gate

Weapon votes count only with a person seen in the last 2 seconds

3 · Corroborate

Level 4 and above need two independent modalities: detector box, scene model, aligned sound

4 · Calibrate

A tool sets each room’s scene thresholds from the device’s own log (demo room: weapon 0.78, fire 0.75)

LevelTCIWhat happens
1 Secure< 0.20Logged
2 Suspicious0.20–0.40Incident on the dashboard; the most an unknown person reaches
3 Elevated0.40–0.65Snapshot, encrypted evidence, SOS request to the owner
4 High0.65–0.85Two modalities agree: dispatch package prepared
5 Critical≥ 0.85Strongly corroborated: the most urgent level

TCI = the strongest confirmed class evidence. Published rules, so every level can be re-derived from the votes in the log.

Our first live run raised about 171 false weapon alarms an hour, every one from a single frame of the scene model just over its threshold. So four rules now stand between a score and an alarm. The TCI is the strongest confirmed evidence, and it maps to five levels with fixed side effects. An unrecognised person is presence, not threat: it stops at Level 2.
